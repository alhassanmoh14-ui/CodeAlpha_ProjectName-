## 1. Introduction

This training introduces participants to phishing attacks and explains how cybercriminals use deception to steal sensitive information.

The training focuses on recognizing suspicious messages, identifying fake websites, understanding social engineering, and applying safe online practices.

## 2. Learning objectives

After completing this training, participants should be able to:

- Define phishing.
- Identify suspicious emails and messages.
- Recognize fake websites and suspicious URLs.
- Explain common social engineering techniques.
- Apply basic cybersecurity practices.
- Respond appropriately to suspected phishing incidents.

## 4. Important teaching notes

### What is phishing?
Explain that phishing involves pretending to be a trusted person or organization to deceive someone.

### Recognizing phishing emails
Teach participants to examine sender addresses, links, unexpected attachments, and unusual requests.

### Fake websites
Explain that a website can use HTTPS and still be fraudulent. Participants should verify the actual domain.

### Social engineering
Explain how criminals exploit fear, urgency, authority, curiosity, and trust.

### Prevention
Encourage unique passwords, MFA, software updates, and independent verification of unusual requests.

## 7. Safety and ethics

This project is strictly for cybersecurity education.

- Do not collect real passwords.
- Do not impersonate real organizations to deceive people.
- Do not distribute malicious links or attachments.
- Do not test phishing techniques against people without authorization.
- Use fictional examples for demonstrations.

## 8. Conclusion

Phishing remains a significant cybersecurity threat because it targets human trust and behavior.

Awareness, careful verification, strong authentication, and timely reporting can reduce the risk of successful phishing attacks.
